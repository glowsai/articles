## What three billable hours would cost

Use the rate to build an initial compute budget:

GPU compute cost = GPU count × hourly rate per GPU × billable hours.

| One GPU, three billable hours | Compute arithmetic | Compute charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L40S | 1 × $0.83 × 3 | $2.49 |
| RTX PRO 6000 | 1 × $2.30 × 3 | $6.90 |
| A100 80GB | 1 × $1.20 × 3 | $3.60 |

These are hypothetical compute examples. The equal duration is an arithmetic assumption, not evidence that the cards finish a task in the same time. Glows.ai does not normally charge separately for data transfer; the main item to add is your Datadrive storage plan. Starting December 1, 2026, Snapshots and images are billed separately per GB-hour rather than counting toward the storage plan.

With per-second billing, you pay only for the seconds an instance runs: compute cost = billable seconds ÷ 3,600 × the hourly rate. Setup, idle time, and retries count as running time, and billing stops when you release the instance, not when your Python process ends.

## Make two rental quotes comparable

Before picking a rate, align five fields:

- GPU identity: model, variant, and memory specification.

- Rental size: GPU count and whether an entire node is required.

- Purchasing terms: on-demand or committed capacity, with any minimums. Glows.ai offers Pay-as-you-go with no minimum rental time, or Subscription for up to 5% off.

- Included resources: CPU, RAM, storage, and network conditions.

- Lifecycle costs: billable states, retained files, transfer, and separately charged services.

A per-GPU headline can require a multi-GPU booking. Use the actual configuration rented. If an offer is priced per node, use that node price directly instead of multiplying it by GPU count again.

Keep price observations dated. A copied figure from a comparison article may describe an older offer, a different rental tier, or an unavailable configuration.
